Many house projects go over budget not because material prices rise, but because there was never a detailed budget plan to begin with. That is where a **RAB** comes in.

## What Is a RAB?

A RAB (*Rencana Anggaran Biaya*, or itemised cost estimate) is a document that sets out the estimated cost of every job in a build or renovation project. The basic principle is simple:

> **Cost of a job = Quantity of work × Unit price of work**

The unit price covers materials, labour, and equipment for one unit of work (for example per m² of wall or per m³ of concrete). As a reference, the Indonesian government publishes unit-price analysis guidelines (AHSP) in [Minister of Public Works Regulation No. 8 of 2023](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/262345/permen-pupr-no-8-tahun-2023), which set material, labour, and equipment coefficients for each type of work.

## Why a RAB Matters

- **Cost control:** you know exactly where the money goes.
- **Comparing quotes:** two contractors can be compared item by item.
- **Basis for the contract and supervision:** work on site is checked against the specifications in the RAB.
- **Preventing disputes:** added or omitted work is clearly recorded.

## What a Complete RAB Includes

| Work group | Example items |
| --- | --- |
| Preparation | Site clearing, batter boards, site office |
| Earthworks & foundations | Excavation, backfill, stone or pad foundations, ground beams |
| Structure | Columns, beams, concrete slab, staircase |
| Walls | Brick or AAC block walls, plaster, skim coat |
| Roof | Roof structure, roofing, gutters |
| Frames & openings | Frames, doors, windows, glass |
| Floors & ceilings | Ceramic/granite tiles, ceiling frame and boards |
| Installations | Electrical, clean water, drainage, septic tank |
| Finishing | Paint, sanitary ware, accessories |

## How to Prepare a RAB Step by Step

1. **Prepare working drawings** (floor plans, elevations, sections, and structural details).
2. **Calculate the quantity of each job** from the drawings—m³ for concrete and excavation, m² for walls and floors, points for installations.
3. **Specify materials precisely**, for example concrete grade, rebar diameter, tile brand and size.
4. **Work out unit prices** from local material and labour prices.
5. **Multiply quantities by unit prices**, then total them per work group.
6. **Add a 10–15% contingency.**
7. **Summarise and review** with the designer or contractor.

## Common Mistakes

- Vague specifications ("standard tiles") that are hard to check.
- Leaving out electrical/plumbing and external works (fence, carport).
- No contingency.
- Design changes carried out without a written RAB revision.

## A Simple Worked Example

Say you are pricing the brick wall on one side of a room:

1. **Calculate the quantity:** wall length 4 m × height 3 m = 12 m², minus a 1.5 m² window opening → **10.5 m²**.
2. **Set the unit price** for brickwork per m² (bricks, cement, sand, and labour) at local prices.
3. **Multiply:** 10.5 m² × unit price = the cost of that wall.

The same logic applies to every item: plaster and skim coat (m²), concrete (m³), rebar (kg), tiles (m²), light points (points), and so on. That is why complete working drawings are a prerequisite for an accurate RAB.

## A Renovation RAB Is Different from a New Build

A renovation RAB needs several items that a new build doesn't:

- **Demolition** of old tiles, walls, ceilings, or roofing.
- **Debris removal** off site.
- **Protection of occupied areas**, such as furniture covers and work paths.
- **Hidden repairs** that only appear after opening up—this is where the contingency comes in.

## How to Read a Contractor's RAB

- **Check that every item has a quantity and unit.** "Lump sum" items without detail are hard to monitor.
- **Compare unit prices** of the main items with local market prices.
- **Make sure specifications are written clearly**, not just "standard" or "equivalent".
- **Check what is excluded:** grid electricity and water connections, fence, garden, or permits.
