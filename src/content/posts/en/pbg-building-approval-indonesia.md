Since **Government Regulation (PP) No. 16 of 2021** was issued under the Job Creation Law, Indonesia's building permit (IMB, *Izin Mendirikan Bangunan*) has been replaced by the **building approval (PBG, *Persetujuan Bangunan Gedung*)**. If you plan to build or renovate a house, here is what you need to know about the PBG.

*Note: technical rules and fees may differ between regions. The legal basis is [PP No. 16 of 2021](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/161846/pp-no-16-tahun-2021); always confirm with the local authority or the official SIMBG portal.*

## What Is a PBG?

A PBG is an approval from the local government allowing the owner to build, alter, extend, reduce, and/or maintain a building in line with technical standards. Unlike the IMB, the PBG focuses on **compliance with building technical standards**, not just permission to build.

## When Does a House Need a PBG?

- **Building a new house.**
- **Renovation that changes the structure**, for example removing columns or beams.
- **Adding floor area or storeys.**
- **Changing the building's function**, for example from a house to business premises.

On the other hand, light maintenance—repainting, replacing tiles, fixing a leaking roof without structural change—generally does not require a PBG.

## What About Houses That Already Have an IMB?

An IMB that has already been issued remains valid. Owners don't have to convert it to a PBG unless they make major changes such as adding a storey or altering the structure—in that case an amended PBG is needed.

## Documents Required for a Residential PBG

1. **Applicant details:** national ID card (KTP) and tax number (NPWP).
2. **Proof of land ownership or rights** (land certificate).
3. **Technical documents:** architectural drawings (floor plans, elevations, sections), structural drawings, and utility plans (electrical, water, sanitation).
4. **Structural calculations** for multi-storey buildings, according to local rules.

## How to Apply for a PBG through SIMBG

1. **Create an account** on the [official SIMBG portal](https://simbg.pu.go.id/).
2. **Enter details** of the owner, land, and building (floor area, number of storeys, height).
3. **Upload** the administrative and technical documents.
4. **Verification and technical consultation** by the local government's assessment team.
5. **Pay the levy** as calculated under local regulations.
6. **The PBG is issued** and can be downloaded digitally.

## Tips for a Smooth Application

- Prepare complete, consistent working drawings from the design stage.
- Make sure the floor area and height you enter match the drawings.
- For an added storey, prepare structural calculations for both the existing building and the new plan.

## PBG vs IMB

| Aspect | IMB (old) | PBG (current) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basis | Permission before building | Approval of compliance with technical standards |
| Application | Varied by region | Centralised through SIMBG |
| Focus | Permission to build | Technical standards, function, and building reliability |
| Follow-up | — | Followed by a Certificate of Fitness for Use (SLF) for certain buildings |

## What Is an SLF?

Besides the PBG, there is also the **Certificate of Fitness for Use (SLF, *Sertifikat Laik Fungsi*)**, which states that a completed building is fit to be used for its intended function. Whether an SLF is required, and its technical rules, depend on the building type and local regulations, so check with the relevant authority.

## Common Mistakes When Applying

1. **Drawings that don't match the plan.** Floor area, storeys, or height in the drawings differ from the data entered.
2. **Incomplete land documents, or documents not in the applicant's name** without supporting papers.
3. **Starting construction before the PBG is issued**, which risks administrative sanctions.
4. **Ignoring setback lines and building coverage/floor-area ratios** (KDB/KLB) that apply to the location.

## How Long Does It Take and How Much Does It Cost?

Processing time and levies vary by regency or city, depending on the building's area, function, and location. The most accurate way to find out is to check the official information on SIMBG and with the local authority before applying.
