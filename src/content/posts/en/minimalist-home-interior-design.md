Smaller homes are now the choice of many urban families. The key is not a bigger building but **smart interior design**. Here are 12 **minimalist home interior design** ideas to make a small house feel more open and comfortable.

## 1. Use a Light, Neutral Palette
White, cream, light grey, and light wood reflect light so rooms feel larger. Add one or two accent colours to keep it from feeling flat.

## 2. Maximise Natural Light
Wide windows, glass doors, or skylights make rooms brighter and save electricity during the day.

## 3. Choose an Open Layout
Combining the living room, dining area, and kitchen without partitions makes circulation feel more generous.

## 4. Multifunctional Furniture
Sofa beds, folding tables, beds with drawers, or extendable dining tables save space without losing function.

## 5. Built-in Storage
Floor-to-ceiling built-in wardrobes make use of vertical space and keep the house looking tidy.

## 6. Use the Space Under the Stairs
The area under the stairs can become a cupboard, bookshelf, or small work corner.

## 7. Mirrors for an Illusion of Space
A large wall mirror reflects light and views, making a room feel twice as big.

## 8. Furniture on Slim Legs
Sofas and cabinets with visible legs make the floor look larger than furniture that sits flat on the ground.

## 9. Layered Lighting
Combine general lighting, task lighting (desk, kitchen), and accent lighting so the space feels warm and has depth.

## 10. High Ceilings or Vertical Lines
Higher ceilings, floor-to-ceiling curtains, or vertical panels make a room feel taller.

## 11. Keep Decoration to a Minimum
Choose a few meaningful pieces—one artwork, a plant, or a favourite object—rather than many small items.

## 12. Indoor Plants
Plants bring freshness and a natural accent without taking up much space.

## Mistakes to Avoid

- Furniture that is too big for the room.
- Too many colours and patterns.
- Ignoring circulation—walkways of around 80–90 cm feel more comfortable.

## Room-by-Room Ideas

### Living and family room
Choose a sofa sized to the room, a light coffee table, and a wall-mounted TV or feature wall with hidden storage. One large rug pulls the seating area together.

### Kitchen
An I- or L-shaped kitchen with upper cabinets up to the ceiling maximises storage. Light cabinet colours and a simple backsplash keep a small kitchen looking clean.

### Bedroom
A bed with storage drawers, sliding wardrobe doors to save swing space, and wall lights instead of bedside lamps.

### Bathroom
Use a glass shower screen instead of a curtain, large-format tiles for fewer grout lines, and a wall-hung cabinet under the basin.

## Planning a Minimalist Interior Step by Step

1. **Measure each room** and note the position of windows, doors, and sockets.
2. **Define how each room is used**—working, studying, receiving guests.
3. **Choose a concept and colour palette** that is consistent throughout the house.
4. **Draw a furniture layout** with comfortable circulation paths.
5. **Visualise it in 3D** before furniture is made, to avoid sizing mistakes.
6. **Set a budget per room** and priorities.

Good daylight and ventilation are not just about looks: Indonesia's housing health standards also set requirements for natural lighting and air exchange in homes (see the references below).
