Before building a house, the most important step is a realistic budget. This guide covers **house construction cost per square meter in 2026** in Greater Jakarta, sample budgets for type 36, 45, and 70 homes, and the extra costs that are often missed.

## Construction Cost per m² in 2026

| Building class | Market range per m² | Typical specification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard | IDR 3 – 4.5M | Standard materials, simple finishes |
| Mid-range | IDR 4.5 – 5.5M | Mid-range materials, neater finishes |
| Premium | IDR 6 – 7M or more | Modern/custom design, premium materials |

*General Greater Jakarta market ranges, not an official quote. Excludes land, design fees, and permits.*

## Sample Budgets for Type 36, 45, and 70 Homes

In Indonesia, a house "type" refers to its floor area in m². Assuming standard to mid-range specification (IDR 3.5–5M/m²):

| House type | Floor area | Estimated building cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Type 36 | 36 m² | ± IDR 126 – 180M |
| Type 45 | 45 m² | ± IDR 158 – 225M |
| Type 70 | 70 m² | ± IDR 245 – 350M |

For a two-storey house, count the total area of both floors. A type 36 built on two floors (for example 36 m² + 36 m²) means 72 m² of floor area, with a cost per m² that tends to be higher because of the structure and staircase.

## What Goes into the Cost

1. **Preparation:** site clearing, setting out, batter boards, and a site office.
2. **Foundation and structure:** excavation, foundations, ground beams, columns, beams, and concrete slabs—the part you should never cut corners on.
3. **Walls and roof:** brick or AAC block walls, plastering and skim coat, roof structure, and roofing.
4. **Frames, doors, and windows.**
5. **Floors, ceilings, and finishes:** tiles, paint, sanitary ware.
6. **Electrical, clean water, and drainage installations.**

## Costs People Often Forget

- **Design and working drawings** (architectural and structural).
- **[PBG building approval](/en/pbg-building-approval-indonesia/)** and local fees.
- **Electricity and water connections.**
- **Fence, carport, and garden.**
- **A 10% contingency** for material price changes or site conditions.

## Tips to Keep Your Budget Under Control

- Use an efficient design: a simple floor plan costs less than one with many corners and curves.
- Fix the material specification early and put it in the [RAB (itemised cost estimate)](/en/house-construction-budget-rab/).
- Compare quotes item by item, not just the total.
- Choose a transparent, experienced contractor—see our [guide to choosing a home contractor](/en/how-to-choose-a-home-contractor/).

## One Storey or Two: Which Is Cheaper?

For the same floor area, a single-storey house is usually cheaper per m² because it doesn't need a concrete slab, a staircase, or a heavier column-and-beam structure. In cities, however, land is often the biggest cost. Building two storeys on a smaller plot can be cheaper overall than buying a larger plot for a single-storey house.

Other considerations:

- **Long-term plans.** If you may add a floor later, design the structure for it from the start (foundations and columns sized for two storeys). This is far cheaper than strengthening the structure later.
- **Who will live there.** Families with elderly members may be more comfortable in a single-storey home.

## Stages and Duration

| Stage | Work involved | Typical duration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Design & permits | Concept, working drawings, cost estimate, PBG | 1–3 months |
| Substructure | Excavation, foundations, ground beams | 2–4 weeks |
| Superstructure | Columns, beams, walls, slab | 1–3 months |
| Roof | Roof structure and covering | 2–4 weeks |
| Finishing | Plaster, floors, ceilings, paint, installations | 1–3 months |

*Duration depends heavily on size, design, weather, and material availability. For a single-storey type 36–70 home, physical construction usually takes a few months.*

## How to Check Quality During Construction

1. **Check the reinforcement** before concrete is poured: bar diameter and spacing must match the structural drawings.
2. **Make sure concrete pouring isn't rushed**, and that the concrete is cured (kept wet) for several days afterwards.
3. **Check that walls are level and square** before plastering.
4. **Pressure-test the plumbing** for leaks before it is covered with tiles.
5. **Photograph every stage**—useful at handover and for future repairs.
