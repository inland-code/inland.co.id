Cleaning skyscraper glass, replacing sealant on the 20th floor, or inspecting a hard-to-reach facade—all of this is now routinely done with **rope access**. So what exactly is rope access, and why do building managers increasingly choose it?

## What Is Rope Access?

**Rope access** is a method of working at height using a **two-rope system**: a *working line* that supports and moves the technician, and a *backup/safety line* that is always attached as a backup. Technicians move up, down, or sideways using specialist equipment such as a harness, descender, ascender, and fall-arrest devices.

## How Rope Access Works

1. **Survey and planning:** identify anchor points, work routes, and risks.
2. **Anchor installation:** anchor points are fixed to strong structural elements on the roof.
3. **Equipment inspection:** every item is checked before use.
4. **Execution:** technicians descend in stages while working on each area.
5. **Supervision:** a supervisor monitors safety and work quality.

## Rope Access vs Scaffolding and Gondolas

| Aspect | Rope access | Scaffolding | Gondola |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Set-up time | Fast | Long (assembly and dismantling) | Moderate |
| Cost | Usually more efficient | High for tall buildings | High if not already installed |
| Access to complex facades | Very flexible | Limited | Limited to the rail track |
| Disruption to building activity | Minimal | Significant | Moderate |

## Work Done with Rope Access

- [Building glass cleaning](/en/building-glass-cleaning-services/) and exterior walls.
- Installing and repairing [building glass sealant](/en/high-rise-glass-sealant-services/).
- Painting and repairing facades, ACP cladding, or panels.
- Visual inspection of facades and structures.

## Rope Access Safety Standards in Indonesia

In Indonesia, work at height is regulated by **[Minister of Manpower Regulation No. 9 of 2016](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/146109/permenaker-no-9-tahun-2016)** on Occupational Safety and Health for Work at Height. It covers worker competency, planning, work procedures, and equipment. Internationally, associations such as **IRATA** issue rope access technician certifications that are widely recognised by the industry.

Key rope access safety principles:

- Always use **two independent ropes**.
- Equipment is inspected and meets standards.
- Technicians are trained and work in teams, never alone.
- A rescue plan is in place before work begins.

## Main Rope Access Equipment

- **Full body harness** to support the technician.
- **Two kernmantle ropes** (working and safety lines) meeting the required specification.
- **Descender** for a controlled descent.
- **Ascender** for climbing up the rope.
- **Back-up device / rope grab** on the safety line that locks automatically in a fall.
- **Anchors and slings** fixed to strong structures on the roof.
- **Helmet, gloves, and other** personal protective equipment.

All equipment must be checked before use and replaced when it shows signs of wear.

## Rope Access Myths and Facts

**"Rope access is more dangerous than a gondola."**
Not necessarily. With two independent ropes, proper procedures, and trained technicians, rope access is designed so that a single equipment failure does not cause a fall.

**"Rope access is only for cleaning glass."**
In fact, it is used for inspection, painting, sealant repair, signage installation, and structural maintenance at height.

**"Rope access disrupts building operations."**
Quite the opposite: because it needs no large structure around the building, lobbies and car parks can usually stay in use, with a safety zone set up below the work area.

## What to Check When Choosing a Rope Access Provider

1. Evidence of technician competency or certification for work at height.
2. A written work plan and rescue plan.
3. Condition and completeness of equipment.
4. Insurance or worker safety cover.
5. Experience with similar buildings.
