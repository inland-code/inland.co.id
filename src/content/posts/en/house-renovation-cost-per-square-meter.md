Renovating is the most realistic way to get a more comfortable home without moving. The first question almost everyone asks is: **how much does a house renovation cost per square meter?** This article summarises 2026 market ranges in Greater Jakarta (Jabodetabek), the factors that drive the cost, and how to keep your budget under control.

## House Renovation Cost per Square Meter in 2026

Renovation is usually priced per square meter (m²) of the area being renovated. Based on ranges commonly used by contractors in Greater Jakarta:

| Renovation level | Typical work | Market range per m² |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light | Repainting, new floor tiles, ceilings, minor repairs | IDR 500k – 1.5M |
| Medium | Kitchen/bathroom renovation, new frames, roof repairs | IDR 1.5 – 3.5M |
| Major / full | Rebuilding, new layout, adding a storey | IDR 3.5 – 6M or more |

*These are general market ranges, not an official quote. The exact price is always set after a site survey.*

As a simple example, a medium renovation of a 40 m² area at an assumed IDR 2.5M/m² comes to around IDR 100M. If the work includes adding a storey, the cost per m² tends to approach that of building new, because it involves structural work.

## What Determines the Cost of a Renovation

### 1. Scope and area of work
Replacing the floor of one room is very different from overhauling a whole house. The more structural work involved (columns, beams, concrete slabs), the higher the cost.

### 2. Condition of the existing building
Older houses often hide problems: structural cracks, seepage, rotten roof timber, or unsafe wiring. These must be fixed before any finishing work.

### 3. Material specification
Tiles, sanitary ware, frames, roof structure, and paint vary widely in price. Material choices alone can change the budget by tens of percent for the same area.

### 4. Labour
For day work, a skilled builder in Greater Jakarta typically earns around IDR 150–200k per day and a helper IDR 110–150k per day, while specialist trades can cost more. Lump-sum contracts are usually easier to control because the cost is agreed per job.

### 5. Permits and neighbourhood rules
Renovation that changes the structure or adds a storey generally requires a [PBG (building approval)](/en/pbg-building-approval-indonesia/). Gated housing estates also often require a renovation permit and set working hours.

## How to Save on Renovation Without Sacrificing Quality

1. **Set priorities.** Fix functional and structural problems (leaks, cracks, wiring) before aesthetics.
2. **Ask for a detailed bill of quantities.** A [RAB (itemised cost estimate)](/en/house-construction-budget-rab/) makes it easy to compare quotes and adjust specifications.
3. **Keep what still works.** Roof structures or frames in good condition don't need replacing just to follow a trend.
4. **Avoid design changes mid-project.** Revisions during construction are the main cause of budget overruns.
5. **Set aside a 10–15% contingency.** Older houses almost always reveal surprises once opened up.
6. **Choose a transparent contractor.** Read [how to choose a reliable home contractor](/en/how-to-choose-a-home-contractor/) before signing a contract.

## Renovate in Stages or All at Once?

Renovating all at once is more efficient because mobilisation, demolition, and clean-up happen only once. Staged renovation still makes sense on a limited budget—as long as the sequence is planned from the start, for example structure and roof first, then interiors.

## Example Estimates by Renovation Type

To make it concrete, here are the jobs homeowners in Greater Jakarta ask for most often. These are illustrations assuming mid-range materials—not quotes.

### Bathroom renovation
A bathroom of about 2 × 2 m usually involves removing old tiles, fixing drainage, waterproofing the floor, new tiles, and replacing the toilet, basin, and shower. Waterproofing is the most often skipped step yet the most important: without it, seepage into the next room or the ceiling below is only a matter of time.

### Kitchen renovation
A kitchen renovation typically covers the countertop (concrete or framed), kitchen cabinets, backsplash, plumbing and electrical work, and an exhaust or cooker hood. The biggest costs are usually the cabinets and the countertop material (granite, solid surface, or tiles).

### Facade renovation
Changing the front of the house—canopy, carport, fence, profile mouldings, exterior paint, or decorative panels—can make a standard house look far more modern without touching the main structure.

### Adding a storey
This has the highest cost per m² because it involves assessing the existing structure, strengthening columns if needed, casting a concrete slab, a staircase, and a new roof. Because it adds floor area and changes the structure, it also generally requires an amended PBG.

## Mistakes That Blow Up a Renovation Budget

1. **No technical survey before setting the budget.** Many problems only appear during demolition.
2. **Treating renovation cost per m² like new-build cost.** Renovation can be more complex because of demolition, adapting the old building, and debris removal.
3. **Buying materials yourself without calculating quantities.** Over- or under-buying both add cost.
4. **Ignoring hidden work** such as old wiring, pipes, and waterproofing.
5. **Choosing the cheapest quote without comparing specifications.** A low price often means thinner work or cheaper materials.

## Checklist Before You Start

- Define the main goal: more space, fixing damage, or a better look.
- Photograph the current condition of the house for your records.
- Get a survey and a detailed cost estimate from at least two contractors.
- Check whether you need a neighbourhood permit or a PBG.
- Plan the schedule: will you live in the house during the works?
- Agree on progress-based payment stages.
